▸ This Week's Feature

Betting $418 Million on Georgia

Investment at this scale is the sort of move that builds real jobs, real wealth, and real personal care products for so many Americans.

In October 2019, two hundred people in a Lock Haven, Pennsylvania ballroom rose to their feet for a man named Kambiz Damaghi. Before he finished speaking, they were on their feet again. He was delivering the keynote at the Clinton County Economic Partnership's annual dinner, not an event known for its standing ovations. But three decades earlier, Damaghi and his family had come from Iran and opened a plant up the road in McElhattan to make diapers. By the night of that dinner, their company employed some 1,700 of the people in that county. You stand up for a man like that.

It’s likely you’ve not heard of him before. But he makes the sort of thing you can’t live without, and he does it from our own shores. Five years after that dinner, they put down $418 million to boost production in Macon, Georgia, rather than targeting a port city internationally.

The company is First Quality, founded in 1989, privately held. It runs out of Great Neck, New York. It got its start with adult incontinence products and became one of those manufacturers that manages to be both enormous and invisible. They have roughly 5,000 employees and plants from Pennsylvania to Georgia; they make diapers, tissues, wipes, and feminine-care products. In 2008, the company bought a private-label diaper operation in Macon and built it into a national brand. The Cuties brand.

The diaper market matters to every parent in the nation, and Cuties holds its own. It's a five-layer build: a cotton-enhanced dryness layer over an absorbent core, which pulls moisture down and holds it, all under a breathable outer cover. The liner is hypoallergenic and dermatologically tested, worked through with aloe, vitamin E, and botanicals. No added fragrance, no dyes, no lotion, no latex, and no elemental chlorine is used in its processing. It is, in plain terms, on par with any premium-brand diaper, made in Lewistown, PA, and Macon, GA.

So where did the $418 million deal come from?

On May 23, 2024, First Quality said it would expand the Macon plant it had operated since 2008: eight new production lines, a 50 percent jump in diaper and training-pant capacity, 600 new jobs, etc, all built right beside the existing operation on Avondale Mill Road. Diapers are bulky, cheap by the unit, and costly to ship, which is exactly the sort of product a financial advisor tells you to make in a place with lower wage costs. First Quality reviewed that same data and poured the money into Georgia anyway. Governor Kemp thanked the company for helping to spur on an economic trend of growth in Georgia. The local mayor had words of his own. Mayor Lester Miller declared, “We are proud to stand with [First Quality] as they invest millions in new infrastructure in our community, meaning hundreds of jobs will be retained and hundreds more created.” The enthusiastic praise is no surprise. Investment at this scale is the sort of move that builds real jobs, real wealth, and real personal care products for so many Americans.

When it comes to diapers, parents tend to balance price against quality, often reaching for bulk sales on quality diapers. By the case (the 108-count boxes a daycare may order), Cuties runs about 30 to 35 cents each, and that tends to run under what a comparable bulk box of Pampers might be. Grab a 25-count pack at full retail and you're closer to 70 cents each, so a bulk purchase makes all the difference in closing the gap for Cuties made domestically. Note, as well, that its American origin is in the grey. Here's the fine print: "responsibly made in the USA with domestic and imported parts." That means some of what goes into the diaper still arrives from elsewhere. But, next to the heavy local investment, it reads more like a conscious trade off than a backroom deal. At the end of the day, they employ hundreds of American workers, and they commit to manufacturing their goods here at home rather than offshoring. If they have to import some materials from overseas to make that possible, maybe it’s a worthy cost.

I like that a family that could have taken the easy exit a dozen times over keeps choosing a mill town in Pennsylvania and a plant in Georgia. Like First Quality chooses to do, the American Buyer can also choose to invest in our home. That’s what it means to be intentional about your choices in the marketplace.

Cuties can be purchased by the case at cuties.com or through various online retailers. Subscription deals are also an option worth considering, especially if you need a steady stream of diapers for a while. Or, just ask whoever runs your daycare to consider stocking their cubbies with Cuties. Someone in Macon made them so that your child could wear them.
