How We Vet a Maker: Inside the HFM Certification Call

From the first phone call to the questions we actually ask, here's how a business earns Bronze, Silver, or Gold under the HFM Certification.

“Hello, this is Faber with Homefront Markets. Am I speaking with the owner?”
“Hey. Yeah this is Nick.”
“It’s nice to meet you … We’ve featured your business and product on our Newsletter and we would like to offer you the opportunity to earn our HFM certification. Would you be willing to answer a few questions about your business and commitment to American Commerce?”
“Go ahead. Shoot.”

As an American Buyer, you care about quality. But you also want to support businesses that create quality in the right way – right here in America. We do a lot of work behind the scenes to ensure that you get both. We want to help you see that process a bit better.

Our team is doing constant research to uncover those hidden gems. When we find a product, company, or founder that stands out for their commitment to American Making and quality products, we get right to work drafting a feature in our newsletter. We want that information brought to your attention as soon as we’re able to bring it to your inbox.

But our evaluation process doesn’t end there. Within a week of publication, our team starts trying to make contact with company management, preferably the owner. We have a specific set of questions – our Maker Questionnaire – designed to help us evaluate them against our HFM Certification standard. Their honest responses to these questions give us the insight we need for a preliminary certification under Bronze, Silver, or Gold.

Certified companies are valuable to us as well as our subscribers. We reach regularly for products to feature in coming newsletters that will promote the business of those that are HFM Certified. This is also part of our regular reassessment of their certified status: with every feature posted to the newsletter, we dive deep into a particular product offered by the company. We scour customer reviews, investigate the materials used in production, and ensure that the certification holds up as they suggested initially. We encourage our readers to assist us in keeping the Makers accountable to their claims.

Occasionally, our first impression is formed on false information. In other cases, a company’s initial commitment and drive for American-based commerce may have drifted or faded over time. Either way, when Homefront Markets discovers the discrepancy, we prioritize a certification revocation announcement on a future issue of the newsletter. We also want this information brought to your attention as soon as we’re able to bring it to your inbox.

You may have a handful of nuanced questions about the HFM Certification we offer. Here are a few common ones we’ll tackle right away, in case you’re also curious about it.

Is the Bronze Certification inherently less American than the Silver Certification? These certifications are not intended to be ranked against one another, since they are awarded on the dual metric system explained in this prior post. These are simply alternative awards to acknowledge steps in the right direction: labor or material. Gold is the biggest success story because they’ve managed to do both.

What about a business that is trying to be an American Maker but doesn’t quite meet the Bronze or Silver standard yet? We reward businesses against clear achievements that are measurable and factual so that our readers, the American Buyers, are not left to wade through the ambiguous. This might mean that Makers who are giving it their best effort are left out of the conversation. But that also means they have something to strive for, and we look forward to featuring them and promoting their business when they make it. Once they have, they can apply for certification at www.homefrontmarkets.com.

Does material from U.S. territories count the same as the 50 states? Yes. If the territory in question is an American territory, growth and success in the commerce of that region helps all of us.

What counts as a U.S. citizen employee? The Department of Labor defines a “U.S. Worker” strictly as a U.S. citizen, U.S. national, permanent resident, refugee, or asylee. So do we.

Do you take money from the businesses you certify? We are always working to ensure we can afford to continue to produce the content our readers need, and we are aiming to launch an exclusive HFM marketplace in the coming weeks. To make this possible, we regularly seek to establish affiliate partnerships with companies featured in our newsletter. With that said, those conversations come after they are first featured in the newsletter, not before. Their willingness to pay us for publicity or marketing services is not a prerequisite to their recognition on Homefront Markets. We want to promote American commerce all across the nation, regardless of whether the business can afford to draw up an affiliate agreement with us.

What if a business disagrees with the certification you gave them? Feedback on certification status is welcomed and encouraged from American Buyers and American Makers alike, and both in the same place: here. Appeals of this kind are reviewed and will always trigger a re-evaluation of the business’ certification status.

In order to be transparent about our process, we thought we would append here the full questionnaire we use in direct communication with a company about their certification status. These are the actual questions we ask. Each question will not always be relevant to any given interview, but we’ll provide you with the comprehensive list for your review.

The Makers Questionnaire

Section 1: Structure (context)

What's the legal name of your business, and where is it headquartered?
Is the company privately owned, and is majority ownership held by U.S. citizens?
Roughly how many people work for the company in total, counting employees and contractors?

Section 2: Workforce

Of your total workforce, how many are classified as employees versus independent contractors or freelancers?
Are all of your employees U.S. citizens? If not, roughly how many are, and what's the status of the rest (permanent resident, visa holder, etc.)?
Does any part of your workforce, including contractors, freelancers, or customer service, work from outside the United States?
Is your product manufactured in-house, or do you outsource production to a third party? If outsourced, where is that manufacturer located, and do you know who they employ?

Section 3: Materials

What are the two or three primary materials that go into your central product?
For each of those materials, where do you obtain them? (country, and state if it's domestic)
Roughly what percentage of your total material cost or weight comes from U.S. sources?
Are there any parts, components, or sub-assemblies in your product that are imported? If so, which ones, and from where?
Where does final assembly or manufacturing take place?

Section 4: Verification

Do you have supplier invoices, certificates of origin, or similar paperwork that could confirm where your materials come from if requested?
Would you be willing to share payroll records or a signed attestation confirming employee citizenship status?
Is there a public statement already on your website or packaging about your hiring or sourcing practices that we could reference?

Section 5: Self-Assessment

Which certification – Bronze, Silver, or Gold – do you believe your business currently qualifies for, and why?
Is there anything about your labor or sourcing that you're actively working to change or improve?

We hope our process to verify and vet the HFM Certification is clear to you. If you have further questions beyond what has been covered here, please don’t hesitate to contact us.
